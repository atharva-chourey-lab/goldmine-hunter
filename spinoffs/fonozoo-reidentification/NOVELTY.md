# Novelty scrub — Fonozoo re-identification (2026-11-18, before any analysis)

Method: `research_novelty_scrub` skill. Claim stated first, split,
each part graded against the closest prior found in the primary
literature and the public record. Searches run 2026-11-18 (EN + ES
terms, widened once).

## The claim as commissioned (one sentence)

"No one has re-identified the Fonoteca Zoológica's (MNCN-CSIC,
Madrid) stranded web audio pile — the 1,013 status-200 audio files
recovered by Goldmine Hunter Campaign 6 — against current
DNA-era anuran taxonomy, and counted how many of its
recording-era species names have changed, been replaced, or been
split."

## Split and graded

**A. The collection and its catalogues exist and are documented —
PUBLIC DOMAIN / BACKGROUND, not the finding.**
Closest priors: the Fonoteca's own pages and history (MNCN site;
FZ 2002–2024 retrospective: 62,424 catalogue records, of which the
published-recordings collection holds 45,892 and the own-recordings
collection 16,532); Márquez's 2004 launch announcement (the two
databases are metadata only — "only the information about the
recordings is included; sound files are not"). The institution
describes and indexes its holdings; it has not published a
taxonomic audit of them.

**B. GBIF collection registrations exist — METADATA ONLY, no
re-identification.**
Closest priors: GBIF.ES collection records registered 2008-08-04 —
"Anfibios Publicados de la Fonoteca Zoológica" (code PUB_AMPHIBIA;
3,866 records, 1,329 species, 85% computerised) and "Anfibios
Propios" (FZ_AMPHIBIA; 5,535 records, 795 species, 50%
computerised). These are collection-level catalogue registrations
(names as catalogued at registration), not occurrence deposits of
the web audio pile and not a name-by-name resolution against a
current backbone. No GBIF occurrence dataset of the Fonozoo audio
files exists.

**C. The sound guides are primary sources, not audits.**
The pile's /sonidos/ files are the tracks of the Fonoteca's own
published sound guides (Spain & Portugal 2004; Bolivia 2002;
Madagascar, Vences/Glaw/Márquez; Cuba; S Brazil & Uruguay, Kwet &
Márquez). Their booklets carry the recording-era names — they are
the *filed* side of the comparison, not a re-identification of it.

**D. A taxonomic re-identification / name-churn count for this
collection — OPEN.**
Widened searches (Fonozoo/Fonoteca + taxonomic revision,
re-identification, Amphibian Species of the World, updated names,
in EN and ES) returned the collection's own descriptive material,
the GBIF.ES registrations, and general bioacoustics-taxonomy
methodology (Köhler et al. 2017, Zootaxa 4251 — which deposits its
own recordings in the *Brazilian* Fonoteca Neotropical, FNJV, not
an audit of Fonozoo). No publication, dataset or catalogue update
was found that resolves the Fonozoo pile's filed names against a
current anuran backbone or counts the changes. The Fonozoo web
catalogue is taxonomically searchable, but no published audit of
its audio filenames' names exists.

## Surviving claim (shrunk to the seam)

Not "the Fonozoo collection is undocumented" — it is documented
and GBIF-registered at collection level. The surviving claim is:

**The first (to the scrub's knowledge) name-by-name taxonomic
re-identification of the Fonoteca Zoológica's stranded web audio
pile: every species-named file in the recovered 1,013-file set,
resolved against a current anuran backbone (GBIF Backbone, 2026),
classified UNCHANGED / MOVED / SYNONYM / SPLIT-flagged, with the
change counted at both unique-name and recording level.**

Scope honesty, registered in advance: this audits *names in
filenames* of the recovered web pile (751 /sonidos guide tracks
plus the smaller accession piles), not the Fonoteca's full
62k-record internal catalogue, most of which has no public audio.
"No first claim beyond this wording is licensed by this scrub."
