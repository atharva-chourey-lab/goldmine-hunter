# Manatee Scar Sheets — novelty scrub (2026-11-18, before any comparison)

Method: `research_novelty_scrub` skill. Claim stated first,
split, each part graded against the closest prior in the
primary literature and the public record. The set itself was
recovered and verified by Goldmine Hunter Campaign 8
(`~/workspace/goldmine-hunter/SHORTLIST-CAMPAIGN8-MAMMAL2-2026-11-18.md`,
graded GOLD under GRADES.md).

## The claim as commissioned (one sentence)

"Joining Wayne Hartley's frozen April-2013 hand-drawn Blue
Spring scar-sheet catalogue (≈200 individuals) to the
post-2013 record to measure how long a sketch-based scar ID
stays re-identifiable — and whether the sheet's own retention
rules predict it — has not been done."

## Split and graded

**A. Blue Spring individual IDs used for population science —
ALREADY PUBLISHED.**
Closest priors: Langtimm, O'Shea et al. (1998), "Estimates of
Annual Survival Probabilities for Adult Florida Manatees" —
mark-recapture survival from photo-documented resightings at
Blue Spring, Crystal River and the Atlantic coast (Blue
Spring adult survival ≈0.96); O'Shea & Hartley, reproduction
and early-age survival at Blue Spring; Reid, Rathbun &
Wilcox (1991), *Marine Mammal Science* 7:180–190 — the
891-individual Florida photo catalogue, 53% resighted. These
works *used* the identification system (its photographic,
MIPS-lineage form) as an instrument. None of them audits the
hand-drawn sheet system itself — its persistence, its error
structure, or its retention rules.

**B. The Blue Spring life histories in published form —
PUBLIC DOMAIN / PUBLISHED BY THE KEEPER.**
Hartley's *Genealogies of Blue Spring Manatees* (Save the
Manatee Club, openly served PDF) publishes the family trees
and life histories derived from the same records, and SMC's
adoptee pages publish updated scar sheets for the ~20+
adoptee animals. This is a published derivative of a subset
— and, for this project, the public outcome ruler (last-seen
and death records for named individuals). It does not
contain the full 2013 sheet set or any persistence analysis.

**C. An audit of the sketch system against the later record —
NARROWED BUT OPEN.**
What no prior found does: take the frozen 2013 Practical
Scar Sheet set as a *test instrument* — transcribe its
≈200 sketch IDs with their scar inventories and first-ID
years, join them to post-2013 outcomes, and measure (i) the
re-identifiability curve (how the probability an animal is
ever resighted again decays with time since 2013), (ii)
whether the sheet's own stated rules — the Practical sheet's
3–5-year drop, the 10-year drop for recognised animals —
predict actual persistence, and (iii) whether persistence
depends on scar load/type as drawn in 2013. FWC's photo-ID
pages show individual photo pairs across years (e.g. TB021,
1993 vs 2006) anecdotally; the drop rules appear in the
sheet's key as practice, never as tested predictions.
Widened searches (scar-pattern persistence, feature
stability, sketch/scar-sheet audits) returned only the
priors in A and B and general photo-ID method chapters —
no persistence audit of any hand-drawn sirenians catalogue.

## Surviving claim (shrunk to the seam)

Not "first use of Blue Spring IDs" — dead, killed by
Langtimm et al. (1998) and Reid et al. (1991). The surviving
claim is:

**The first persistence audit of a hand-drawn scar-ID
catalogue: Hartley's frozen April-2013 Blue Spring sheet set,
transcribed and joined to post-2013 records, measuring the
re-identifiability curve of sketch-based scar IDs and
testing the sheet's own 3–5-year / 10-year drop rules as
predictions.**

"No first claim beyond this wording is licensed by this
scrub." One structural caveat, registered with the claim:
the full outcome ruler (MIPS) is researcher-only; the
registered design runs on the public ruler first (part B)
and treats MIPS as a formal data request, not an
assumption.
